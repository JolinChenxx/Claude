# Molten copper ejection: observations and limits

High fluence (105 J/cm2, 0.4 mJ): Fig. 4e1 reports 344 m/s for the rise of the spatter top. The height stops increasing after about 500 ns. Low fluence (42 J/cm2, 0.32 mJ): about 190 m/s. These are edge-height observations, not individual droplet tracks or a velocity distribution. See SI physical page 1, Table 1, extraction lines 39-61 for irradiation conditions.

The authors compare pump-probe images of spatter with ultrashort-exposure plume images. They interpret the overlap with the bright plume centre as evaporation. The text does not establish continuous tracking of a single drop from 100 to 500 ns, a launch angle, or an in-flight droplet diameter. Deposited nanoparticle sizes and the plotted spatter height are different quantities. No image positions, slopes or sizes were measured for this note.
